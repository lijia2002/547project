### Q3. What caused the marked increase in global temperature that began after 1970?
The marked temperature increase after 1970 was caused by the growing accumulation of greenhouse gases combined with controls on sulfur pollution. Prior to this period, the warming impact of greenhouse gases had been offset by the cooling effect of sulfate aerosols from sulfur dioxide emissions, which reflect sunlight. Once sulfur pollution was controlled, this offsetting cooling effect was reduced, leading to a significant rise in temperature.

## Neural_network
**Source Type:** Wiki

### Q1. What is an artificial neural network?
An artificial neural network is a computational model in machine learning that is inspired by the structure and functions of biological neural networks. It consists of interconnected nodes, called artificial neurons, which are typically organized into layers. These neurons receive, process, and send signals to other connected neurons, with the connections having weights that adjust during the learning process.

### Q2. What were some of the foundational contributions to neural networks made by early researchers like McCulloch, Pitts, and Hebb?
In 1943, Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts created a non-learning computational model for neural networks and developed mathematical models of artificial neurons. In the late 1940s, D. O. Hebb proposed a learning hypothesis based on neural plasticity known as Hebbian learning, which was subsequently used in many early neural networks.

### Q3. What major limitations led to a decline in interest in early neural network research?
Early neural network research declined due to several major limitations, including hardware constraints that limited network size and training efficiency, as well as an incomplete theoretical understanding of learning algorithms. A key issue was that many early models, such as single-layer perceptrons, were restricted to solving only linearly separable problems. These limitations were highlighted in the book *Perceptrons* by Marvin Minsky and Seymour Papert, which contributed to reduced research interest in the late 1960s and 1970s.
